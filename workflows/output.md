FINAL VERSION: The Kanto Region: Geography and Structure in Pokémon
================================================================================

Kanto, Pokémon’s original game region, debuted in Pokémon Red and Green in Japan in 1996. Named after and geographically inspired by Japan’s Kantō region, it introduced a setting whose landscapes and institutions shaped the franchise’s development.

Its fictional geography links settlements through routes crossing forests, mountains, and coastal waters. Small Pallet Town contrasts with Saffron City, an urban crossroads connecting surrounding cities. This network integrates rural and metropolitan spaces, adapting rather than reproducing the geography of its Japanese namesake.

Within this landscape, research and competition organize gameplay. Capturing creatures and recording species in the Pokédex, an electronic encyclopedia, support Professor Oak’s research. Eight Gym Badges, earned by defeating Gym Leaders, mark competitive achievement and qualify players for the Pokémon League, the region’s championship institution. Alongside these structured activities, Team Rocket, a criminal organization that exploits Pokémon, introduces narrative conflict that contrasts coercive practices with research and regulated competition.

These spatial and institutional patterns underpin Kanto’s lasting franchise significance. Associated with the original 151 Pokémon, the region later returns in Gold and Silver and in remakes of its debut games. Such appearances reinforce its historical importance, while its interconnected geography and progression systems established a foundational model for subsequent Pokémon regions.
